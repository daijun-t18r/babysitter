# Knowledge: Colic & gas

Canonical facts:
- Colic (rule of 3s): crying 3+ hours/day, 3+ days/week, 3+ weeks, in an otherwise healthy, growing baby. Peaks ~6 weeks, typically gone by 3-4 months. Cause unclear; not the parent's fault; baby is not in danger.
- Gas is mostly swallowed air: fast letdown, bottle flow too fast, crying itself.
- Evidence for gas drops (simethicone) and gripe water is weak; simethicone is considered safe. Probiotics show mixed evidence. Never "colic remedies" containing alcohol or unlisted herbs.

Try this first:
- 0-3m: bicycle legs + gentle clockwise tummy massage; burp mid-feed and after; football hold (tummy over forearm); check bottle nipple flow isn't too fast; paced feeding.
- 3-6m: true colic fades by now — persistent evening fussing is more often overtiredness; try an earlier, darker wind-down.
- 6-12m: gas complaints usually track new solids; note trigger foods and mention patterns to the pediatrician.

Red flags: crying with vomiting (especially green or forceful), blood or mucus in stool, fever, a distended hard belly, or a baby who cannot be consoled at all for hours → doctor. Colic exhausts parents — putting the baby down safely and stepping away is always allowed.
