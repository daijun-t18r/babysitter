# Knowledge: Night survival (the parent matters too)

Canonical facts:
- Sleep deprivation at this level is a physiological injury, not a willpower failure. It measurably impairs judgment and mood, and it makes 3am feel catastrophic. Naming this helps.
- It is ALWAYS safe and always allowed to put the baby down in a bare crib, on their back, and step out to breathe for a few minutes — a crying baby in a safe crib is safe; an exhausted parent holding a baby on the edge of sleep on a couch is not.
- Shift splitting works: two parents alternating full 3-4 hour blocks (one fully off duty, earplugs and all) beats both being half-awake all night. A bottle of expressed milk or formula makes the off-shift real.
- Feeding while profoundly sleepy on a sofa or armchair is the highest-risk fall-asleep scenario — feed sitting up in bed, or stand and walk.
- Postpartum mood and anxiety conditions are common (~1 in 5 mothers, fathers too) and treatable: National Maternal Mental Health Hotline 1-833-852-6262, and 988 in crisis.

Try this first:
- Tonight: pick the ONE next feed someone else can take; lower the bar — a fed baby in a safe crib and a parent who rested 3 hours is a successful night.
- This week: institute shifts; protect one 4-hour block per parent per night.

Red flags: a parent describing hopelessness, rage at the baby, thoughts of harm, or "I can't do this anymore" → this is the crisis protocol, warm and immediate, numbers in the text.
