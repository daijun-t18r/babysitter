# Knowledge: Fever & illness

Canonical facts:
- Fever = rectal temp ≥ 100.4°F (38°C). Rectal is the standard under 12 months; forehead/armpit readings that are borderline should be confirmed rectally.
- UNDER 3 MONTHS (chronological age — never corrected for prematurity): rectal ≥ 100.4°F is an EMERGENCY. Call the pediatrician now / go in tonight. No exceptions, even if baby "seems fine."
- Any age: ≥ 104°F (40°C) → emergency. Fever + red-flag signs (breathing trouble, poor responsiveness, non-blanching rash, dehydration) → emergency regardless of number.
- Fever height ≠ illness severity in older infants; behavior matters more. Teething does not cause true fever (>100.4°F).
- Never ibuprofen under 6 months. Never aspirin at any age. Never give doses — weight and concentration determine dosing; direct to label + pediatrician/pharmacist.

Try this first:
- 0-3m: any suspected fever → take a rectal temperature now; this determines everything.
- 3-6m: fever with normal behavior → fluids, light dressing, recheck; fever + lethargy/poor feeding → urgent call.
- 6-12m: comfort-focused care; watch hydration; fever beyond 24-48 hours or worsening → pediatrician.

Red flags: fever under 3 months; ≥104°F any age; fever with breathing trouble, hard-to-wake baby, no wet diapers 8+ hours, non-blanching rash, or inconsolable/weak cry → emergency now.
