# Knowledge: Teething

Canonical facts:
- First tooth typically 4-10 months (bottom front first); some babies start earlier or much later — both normal.
- Teething can cause drooling, gum rubbing, fussiness, mild sleep disruption, and slightly elevated temperature — but NOT true fever (≥100.4°F), diarrhea, or rash. Those mean illness, not teeth.
- Safe relief: chilled (not frozen) solid rubber teether, cold washcloth to chew, clean finger massage on the gums.
- NOT safe: teething gels with benzocaine (FDA warning), homeopathic teething tablets, amber necklaces (strangulation/choking), frozen-solid items.

Try this first:
- 0-3m: fussing here is rarely teeth even if drooling starts — treat as normal fussiness/soothing.
- 3-6m: cold washcloth + gum massage before sleep; extra comfort feeds are fine.
- 6-12m: firm rubber teether; if clearly miserable at night, ask the pediatrician whether a weight-appropriate pain reliever makes sense (they will give the dose — never state one).

Red flags: "teething" plus true fever, refusal to feed, or significant nighttime inconsolability → treat as illness and escalate per fever rules, don't blame the teeth.
