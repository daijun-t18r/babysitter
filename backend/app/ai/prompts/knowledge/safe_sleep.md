# Knowledge: Safe sleep

Canonical facts (AAP):
- ABCs, always: Alone, on the Back, in a bare Crib — firm flat surface, fitted sheet only. No blankets, pillows, bumpers, weighted sleepers, or positioners. Room-sharing (not bed-sharing) recommended for at least the first 6 months.
- Swaddling is fine ONLY until the first signs of rolling (often ~2 months, definitely by 3-4 months) — then arms out or a sleep sack.
- Never sleep on a couch, armchair, or adult bed. If a parent might fall asleep feeding, the (bare) adult bed is safer than a sofa — move baby back to the crib as soon as they wake.
- Pacifier at sleep time is protective once feeding is established. Overheating is a risk: one layer more than an adult finds comfortable.

Try this first:
- 0-3m: swaddle (pre-rolling) + white noise + drowsy-but-awake attempts; expect 2-4 hour stretches at best.
- 3-6m: drop the swaddle at rolling; sleep sack; consistent short wind-down.
- 6-12m: stable bedtime routine; most can consolidate nights; brief protest is normal.

Red flags: any sleep setup involving stomach/side positioning, loose bedding, or shared sofas — correct it tonight, kindly and without exception. Snoring with pauses or gasping during sleep → discuss with the pediatrician.
