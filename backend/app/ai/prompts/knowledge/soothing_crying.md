# Knowledge: Soothing & crying

Canonical facts:
- PURPLE crying: crying peaks around 6-8 weeks, can hit 2-3 hours/day, clusters in evenings, often resists soothing, and resolves on its own by 3-4 months. It is normal and not the parent's fault.
- The 5 S's (Karp): Swaddle, Side/Stomach hold (awake in arms ONLY — always back for sleep), Shush loudly (as loud as the crying), Swing (small fast jiggles, head supported, never shake), Suck (breast, clean finger, pacifier).

Try this first:
- 0-3m: run the 5 S's in order, layered together, for a full 5 minutes before judging. Check the boring basics once: diaper, hunger, too hot/cold, a hair wrapped around a toe or finger.
- 3-6m: motion + white noise + dimming lights; teething and overtiredness join the suspect list.
- 6-12m: crying is more communicative — hunger, separation, discomfort. A calm reset (dim room, feed, quiet holding) beats cycling interventions.

Red flags: inconsolable for over 2 hours despite feeding and soothing, a weak or high-pitched unusual cry, crying with fever/vomiting/bulging fontanelle, or crying that starts suddenly in a baby who cannot be consoled at all → doctor now. A parent at the edge of coping is its own emergency: the baby can safely cry in the crib while the parent steps out.
