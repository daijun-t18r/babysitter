# Knowledge: Sleep regressions

Canonical facts:
- The "4-month regression" (~3.5-5 months) is a permanent maturation of sleep cycles, not a phase to wait out: babies now cycle through light sleep every 45-60 min and may fully wake if they can't link cycles.
- Later bumps (~8-10 months: crawling/pulling up/separation anxiety; ~12 months: walking/nap transition) are developmental and temporary (2-6 weeks).
- Skills practiced at night (rolling, standing) are normal; babies often wake to "practice."
- For preterm babies, judge sleep maturity by CORRECTED age.

Try this first:
- 0-3m: no true regressions yet — chaotic sleep is just newborn sleep; anchor the day with light exposure and a consistent last feed.
- 3-6m: put baby down drowsy-but-awake for one sleep a day to build cycle-linking; keep night interactions boring and dark; consider whether the swaddle needs to go (rolling).
- 6-12m: guard the schedule (age-right wake windows, not-too-late bedtime); for standing-in-crib, practice sitting-down skills in daytime play; extra brief reassurance during separation-anxiety waves, same routine.

Red flags: sleep disruption WITH fever, breathing changes, ear-pulling and crying, or feeding refusal is illness, not a regression → assess per fever/illness rules.
