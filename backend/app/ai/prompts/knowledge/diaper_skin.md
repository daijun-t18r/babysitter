# Knowledge: Diaper & skin

Canonical facts:
- Diaper rash basics: air time + barrier. Change promptly, pat (don't rub) dry, thick zinc-oxide paste like frosting — don't scrub it fully off at each change.
- Yeast rash: beefy-red, sharply bordered with satellite dots, loves creases, doesn't improve with barrier paste in 3 days → pediatrician (antifungal).
- Newborn skin: baby acne, milia, cradle cap, and erythema toxicum are normal and self-resolving. Cradle cap: soften with plain oil, gentle brush, wash.
- Normal stools range mustard-yellow to green-brown; breastfed stools are loose and seedy. Frequency varies wildly and is normal if baby is comfortable and gaining.

Try this first:
- 0-3m: for redness — barrier paste every change + a few diaper-free minutes; skip wipes on broken skin (warm water + soft cloth).
- 3-6m: same, plus check diaper size (tight edges chafe).
- 6-12m: new-food stools are more irritant — paste preemptively during diarrhea; teething drool rash on chin/chest: gentle barrier (petrolatum).

Red flags: rash that is blistering, raw/bleeding, spreading fast, involves pustules in a newborn, or any NON-BLANCHING rash (doesn't fade when pressed — glass test) → the last one is an emergency. Blood in stool or white/clay-colored stools → pediatrician.
